# Abstract
Pleural mesothelioma (PM) is an aggressive malignancy characterized by marked histological and molecular heterogeneity, particularly in biphasic tumors. While prior studies suggest that PM subtypes exist along a molecular continuum, the spatial organization of tumor states remain poorly defined.

Here, we integrate single cell and spot level spatial transcriptomics (ST), trajectory inference, scRNA-seq derived copy number variation, metabolic pathways analysis, and multi-platform validation across 20 epithelioid and 3 biphasic PM patients to characterize spatial molecular plasticity in PM. We identify transcriptionally and spatially distinct tumor states arranged in a radial architecture within a biphasic tumor, including an intermediate population enriched for proliferation and epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) programs. These states align along a continuous transcriptional axis accompanied by gradual morphological changes from epithelioid to sarcomatoid phenotypes and share a common malignant clone. 

These tumor states were further detected in an additional cohort of 23 surgically resected PM samples profiled using both single cell and multicellular whole transcriptome ST approaches. Inferred cell-cell communication analysis reveals subtype specific microenvironment signaling and metabolic features, including AXL and CD70 in biphasic tumors and VEGFR2 in epithelioid tumors. Together, these findings provide a spatial framework for understanding tumor heterogeneity in PM and establish a foundation for future functional and translational studies.
